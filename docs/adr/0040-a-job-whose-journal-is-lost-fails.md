# A job whose journal is lost fails rather than starting over

A Runner carries a job on from its journal (ADR 0026). A crash leaves the journal intact, but other events do not: the job's folder deleted while the Runner is stopped, a partly lost disk, a data folder restored from an older backup, or a host's power cut, after which SQLite in WAL mode with `synchronous = NORMAL`, as pi-durable opens it, may roll back its last commits. A missing journal opened as before became an empty one, and pi-durable started the job over from its Input: it wrote the Input files again, ran every completed tool call again among the first run's files, and repeated its model spend. A journal behind the hub carried on from its earlier point and repeated every tool call since, and its new records could take the IDs of ones the hub holds, with other content.

So a Runner does not carry on a started job from a journal that is missing or behind the hub: the job fails with the reason `journalLost`, whose message says what the Runner found, such as "The Runner has no journal; the hub holds events up to ID 412." Detection needs no new field. Every run carries the hub's position, the ID of the last record it stored, and above 0 the hub holds the job's start, so a journal known to be absent is lost. A journal that exists must hold the exact record at that position: IDs have gaps and are shared across pi's records, so a large enough last ID proves nothing. The check comes before anything writes the job's records, since opening pi-durable's Harness recovers its work, which writes: it opens the Harness read-only, which recovers nothing, closes it and opens it again to carry on. Opening the journal's SQLite file still applies pi's schema migrations, which keep every record and its ID, and checkpoints its write-ahead log on closing; pi-durable 1.5.2 cannot open its SQLite storage read-only, as the migrations write. Every open checks, the job's run, its recovery, its resume and the recording of its pause or cancel, also one of a journal this process opened before. Nothing is deleted: the job's Workspace stays for inspection, and a pause or cancel of such a job fails it rather than take the path of a job that never started, which deletes its files.

The failure travels beside the events, with none and at the hub's position, as what a lost journal holds is not the history the hub has. The hub keeps the job's events up to that position, its usage and its start. A journal whose lookup fails, other than by not existing, no longer counts as one that may exist, which started the job without its Input files: the Runner drops the run, and the hub's next answer, which lists it again, tries again, as for a pause or cancel that cannot be recorded. Resuming the job stays allowed: it carries on if an operator restored the journal, and otherwise fails again. Starting over is a new job, submitted under a new custom ID, with its own journal, events and usage.

`journalLost` joins the failure reasons within the protocol's version: their description already tells callers that new ones come. The hub ships before the Runners, as an older hub refuses the reason, and an older Runner skips the check, which leaves it as before.

## Considered options

- Starting the job over from its Input: a tool call can act outside the job, and a silent rerun repeats it, adds two runs' usage up as one, and never tells the caller.
- A digest of the record at the hub's position, carried in the protocol and compared on every report: a journal goes back only while its Runner is down, and every open checks before writing, so a journal behind the hub is caught before any of its IDs is used again, and one at or past the hub's position holds every record the hub has. Reconsider if any open can skip the check.
- Holding each new job until the hub acknowledges its start: the start is reported as soon as the journal opens, before the first model call, so a loss inside that gap is rare, and the hold would add a round trip to every job.
- A reason each for a missing journal, one behind the hub and one that cannot be looked up: the caller does the same for each, and the message says which. A lookup that fails is retried instead.
- Refusing to resume such a job: an operator can restore its journal, and the hub cannot know a host's disk.
- A new protocol version: an older Runner, which skips the check, does no worse than before.

## Consequences

Each run of a started job opens its journal twice. A rollback that loses only records the hub never received passes the check, and the job may repeat those last seconds of work; closing that gap needs `synchronous = FULL`, a sync of the WAL on every commit, where the Runner keeps the `NORMAL` that `openNodeSqliteStorage` sets. Effects outside the job happen exactly once only if the effects themselves are idempotent. A journal that cannot be looked up keeps its job running, retried with every answer, until it can. A reported `journalLost` counts in its Batch's failure breaker, as `runFaulted`, the other fault on the Runner, does: neither says anything about the Batch's content, but a host whose jobs fail so pauses their Batches until someone looks.
